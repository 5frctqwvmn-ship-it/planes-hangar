# "A Picture of the Work" - script v2 (3 minutes)
Rewritten from MJ's notes on proof cut v3 (Nov 2, 8:45 PM). Target 3:00 total, hard cap 3:10.
The one question the film answers: "What is my crew of agents actually doing?" Every section moves toward that answer. Cut anything that does not.

Host: HOIST, the forklift. Bubbly, whimsical but informative, a warm drawl, an upbeat cartoon teacher (in the spirit of the classic animated DNA explainer, not copying it). He PAUSES: a beat after every sentence, a longer beat before each new idea. Eyes and mouth synced to the words.
Pointing: Hoist pops out of his corner box and points with a long pointer stick held in his forks, like a teacher at a projector wall. The tip touches the thing he is naming; a small ripple marks it.
Music: a different short cue per section (5 cues), matched to the mood, never looping long enough to get repetitive.

---

## 1. Cold open: the game (0:00 to 0:18)
VISUAL: Real gameplay, fast and colourful: painted planes racing, El Chupacabra in the pack, a win show, then the finished airport runway with the ground crew. No before/after.
MUSIC: bright, adventurous.
HOIST (pops in from the side): "Howdy! [beat] This here is Planes Hangar. [beat] A plane game for one four-year-old pilot. [long beat] And it was built by a whole crew of AI agents."

## 2. The problem (0:18 to 0:32)
VISUAL: Black screen, one terminal line with a blinking cursor: "Running agent... 2h 23m". It just sits there.
MUSIC: drops to a single curious plucked line.
HOIST: "Trouble is, [beat] while they work, this is all you get. [beat] Running. [beat] Running what? [long beat] So we built a way to watch."

## 3. One plane, up close (0:32 to 1:20)
VISUAL: The finished board, but framed on ONE busy plane filling the screen: every vehicle around it working, beacons on, a camera truck with a CPU pill, a briefing arc arriving. Hoist steps out with his pointer. He touches each thing as he names it. Pull back to the full field only at the end.
MUSIC: light, playful, "explainer" rhythm.
HOIST:
- (plane) "Every plane is one agent at work. [beat]"
- (fuel truck) "The fuel truck? That's it thinking. [beat]"
- (baggage cart) "Baggage is reading files. [beat]"
- (tug) "The tug runs commands. [beat]"
- (camera truck, CPU pill) "And that camera truck is filming. [beat] See the number? That's how hard the computer is working. [beat]"
- (beacon / lights) "Beacon on, it's busy. Green, doing a step. Amber, thinking. [beat]"
- (tab) "And the tag tells you what it's costing. [long beat]"
- (pull back; tower) "Up top, the orchestrator. [beat] It's the director. It reads your request, plans the work, and briefs each agent. [beat]"
- (tower's own carts) "These little carts by the tower? That's the director's own work: thinking, reading, writing the briefs. [beat]"
- (briefing arc from tower to a plane) "When it hands out a job, you see the order fly down the taxi line. [beat]"
- (dish, a pulse from a plane to the dish to the board) "And the radio listens to everybody, [beat] and keeps this board up to date, every few seconds. [beat] It's the cheapest agent we have, so the director never wastes its time on that. [long beat]"

## 4. How we got here (1:20 to 2:22)
STRICTLY IN ORDER, oldest to newest, each version moving, a small "V3 of 29" label. Never jump back to a finished version mid-sequence. About 4 to 6 s per beat.
MUSIC: building, "assembly" cue that grows as the board grows.
HOIST:
- (V1 planes on the field) "It started simple. [beat] Planes for agents. You could see them. [beat] You couldn't see what they were doing."
- (trucks, then the tower) "They became ground crew. [beat] The orchestrator got a tower."
- (radio appears) "A cheap radio kept it up to date. [beat]"
- (HD trucks, panels montage, fast) "Better trucks. Usage bars. A phone fit."
- (beat, music dips) "Then the real question. [beat] What are they DOING?"
- (little people) "So every tool call got its own little worker."
- (flat carts, the clutter) "Then carts. [beat] Lots of carts. [beat] It looked hectic."
- (icons, beacons, arcs: the unlock, hold 8 s) "So we organized the chaos. [beat] An icon for each kind of work. A light for working or waiting. [beat] And for the first time, you could say: that one's reading, that one's filming."
- (round pads, tower to the top, airfield) "Pads, so you know whose cart is whose. [beat] A real airfield."
- (back to planes) "And the agents became planes again. [beat] Started with planes, [beat] ended with planes. [beat] Now with a crew you can watch."

## 5. One day, sped up (2:22 to 2:42)
VISUAL: The final board replaying the day at 150x with all dead time cut (long idle stretches removed so something is always happening). Clock in the corner. For the last 4 s a pointer tap on the video agent's plane: "That's this video being made."
MUSIC: energetic, the biggest cue.
HOIST: "Here's one Sunday, [beat] sped up. [long beat] It looks hectic. [beat] But it's organized chaos. [long beat] And that plane? [beat] That's me, making this video."

## 6. Close (2:42 to 3:00)
VISUAL: Zoom in on Hoist's face. A small pop-up plane-bar graph of the day's cost by model appears beside him and pops away.
MUSIC: warm, resolving.
HOIST: "It all started with plain curiosity. [beat] What's going on under the hood? [long beat] Now it's how we track the spend, [beat] and keep the crew honest. [long beat] Thanks for riding along, partner. [beat] Go check on your crew!"
VISUAL: Hoist waves a fork. Large credit: "Made by MJ and his son, with an orchestrator and a crew of agents."

---
PRONUNCIATION: avoid heteronyms the voice can misread ("live", "read", "lead", "wind", "close"). Rephrase ("up to date", "running now", "reading files" is fine as a gerund) or respell for the voice only (e.g. "lyve" for the adjective). No mention of "the cloud" anywhere: say "the board" instead. Listen-check: run the voice output through a quick speech-to-text pass (e.g. pip faster-whisper tiny) and compare with the script to catch mispronounced or skipped words.
